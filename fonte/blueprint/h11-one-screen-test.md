# The one-screen test
Run it on every version. Answer yes or no. Then count the parts. The version with the fewest parts and no "no" wins.

Built from Power Design web rules 5, 6 and 19, and the ideas in Steve Krug's book "Don't Make Me Think". No text from the book.

## Ten checks
1. One job. Can you say what this screen is for in ten words or fewer?
2. Five seconds. Show it for five seconds. Can a stranger say what it is, who it is for and what to do next?
3. One main button. Is there exactly one filled, accent-coloured button on the screen, 44px tall or more?
4. Clickable looks clickable. Do buttons and links look pressable? Does plain text look like plain text?
5. Labels. Does every field have a visible label, not only a placeholder?
6. Fewest fields. Does every field earn its place for today's job? Could you ask for it later?
7. Plain words. Does a stranger know every word at once?
8. Nothing extra. Remove one item that does not help the job. Does the job still work?
9. Familiar places. The logo at top left goes home. Menus sit where people expect them.
10. Phone. At 390px wide, can you finish with one thumb and no sideways scroll?

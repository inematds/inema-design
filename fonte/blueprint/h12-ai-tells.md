# AI tells checklist (short)
From the catalogue in Jack's Slop Monster repo (github.com/ItsssssJack/SlopMonster, references/signs-of-ai-writing.md). That catalogue draws on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing". People write some of these too. Read each match in context. Do not edit by rote.

## 1. Words: cut them, or swap for a plain word
delve, tapestry, testament to, seamless, robust, navigate the landscape, in today's fast-paced world, it's important to note, unlock, supercharge, elevate, game-changing, harness the power of, dive deep, myriad, plethora, realm, ever-evolving, cutting-edge, transformative, paradigm shift, synergy, holistic, embark.
Check in context: leverage, foster, crucial, pivotal, streamline, empower, showcase, curated, compelling, innovative, comprehensive, journey, solution, effortless, intuitive, world-class, boost.
Fix: a plain word. "Leverage" becomes "use".

## 2. Shapes
- "Not just X, but Y" and "more than just": cut the first half, keep the claim.
- "Whether you're X or Y": name the one reader.
- "That's where X comes in": say what X does.
- "Say goodbye to": name what replaces it.
- "Imagine a": show it instead.
- Stacked hedges such as "may potentially": keep one at most.
- A question that answers itself ("The result? Faster."): write a sentence.
- A closing summary nobody asked for. A bold lead on every bullet.

## 3. Punctuation
- Two or more em dashes in one sentence.
- Four hyphenated compounds in one sentence.
- Many semicolons.

## 4. Rhythm
- A list of exactly three ("faster, smarter, better"). Keep a list only when every item adds something.

## 5. Proof
- Any number, user count, rating or quote you cannot show. Write [needs number] and ask the owner.

## Last check
- Does the first line answer the reader's first question?
- Does every sentence add something?
- Is every claim true, and can you show it?
- Would you say it out loud to a friend?
- Did you keep the writer's meaning?
A score of 5/5 only means no scored pattern matched. It does not prove the copy is clear or true.
